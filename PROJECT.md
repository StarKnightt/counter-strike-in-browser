# cs2-dust2 — a browser FPS on a CS2-inspired Dust II A-site

Project write-up. Everything below is grounded in the files that are in the repository (`package.json`, `index.html`, `src/**`, `tools/**`, `public/**`, `assets_src/**`). Where a claim cannot be verified from the repo (things that happened in chat sessions, skill usage, numbers that were read off a profiler and never written down) it is marked as such.

## TL;DR

- Single-player first-person shooter running in the browser: Three.js r185 + three-mesh-bvh, bundled by Vite, no framework, no React, ~7,500 lines of JS in `src/`, ~5,100 lines of Blender Python in `tools/`, ~800 lines of Playwright harnesses.
- One map (Dust II A-site: long, long doors, pit, car, site, goose, ninja, triple, ramp, CT spawn, short/catwalk, stairs), one bombsite, three weapons (AK-47, AWP, knife), five bots, one round at a time with CT/T side alternation, plant/defuse/explode, HUD with radar, killfeed, Valorant-code crosshair.
- All geometry (map, weapons, hands, C4, bots' bodies and clothing) is built procedurally by Python scripts executed in Blender and exported to GLB. No Valve assets, nothing from Source2Viewer, no downloaded models from Sketchfab / Poly Pizza / Hyper3D / Hunyuan3D.
- Not everything is home-made, and this should be said plainly: the map's PBR textures are CC0 scans from Poly Haven (fetched by `tools/fetch_textures.py`, re-graded by `tools/grade_textures.py`); the sound one-shots are CC0 recordings from Freesound (listed in `public/audio/CREDITS.txt`, sliced by `tools/slice_audio.py`); the bots' skeleton and Idle/Walk/Run clips come from the Mixamo-rigged `Soldier.glb` (the three.js example character), with the body generated by the MPFB2 (MakeHuman) Blender add-on; the HUD font is Barlow Semi Condensed (`public/fonts`). Weapon textures, grime, decal sheets, muzzle flashes, bullet holes, the sky, and the voice lines are generated (JS canvas / GLSL / PIL / Windows TTS).
- Rendering: MSAA HDR render, GTAO, bloom, sun shafts, filmic grade, custom fixed-disk PCF shadows with receiver-plane bias, adaptive render scale, shader warm-up, shadow-map refresh gating.
- Testing was done with headless Playwright (Chromium on ANGLE/D3D11) harnesses: per-system tests, review screenshots from fixed cameras, a scripted round recorded to video, fps probes.

I want you to build a playable first-person shooter set on a CS2-inspired Dust2 bombsite. This should look and feel like a real Counter-Strike round. Not a tech demo, not a vibe-coded sketch. A full playable round where someone watching the video thinks "this is actually a game."

One bombsite (A site, Dust2-inspired). Sandy Middle Eastern architecture. Concrete walls with bullet marks and wear. Wooden crates and barrels for cover. The iconic ramp, the site box positions, the pit area. Bright harsh desert sun, hard shadows. Dust particles in the air. The skybox is washed-out blue desert sky.

The map is small but detailed. Every surface should look like CS2 — not low-poly, not stylized, not sketch art. Photoreal sandy concrete, weathered wood, rusted metal, faded paint. When you pause the video, it should look like a screenshot from the actual game.

## Blender MCP workflow

Use the Blender MCP connection to build the map geometry. This is NOT procedural generation like my previous builds — this time use Blender to model the actual environment:

1. Build the bombsite layout in Blender — walls, ramp, boxes, barrels, site floor, elevated positions
2. UV unwrap and apply procedural materials IN Blender — sandy concrete, wood planks, metal, painted walls
3. Bake lighting if possible for extra realism
4. Export as glTF/GLB for Three.js import
5. Set up colliders and navmesh data from the Blender geometry

The Blender map is the foundation. Everything else (weapons, bots, effects, sound) is built in Three.js code on top of the imported map.

## Weapons

3 weapons only. No more.

1. **AK-47** — automatic, recoil pattern that pulls up and left, 30 round mag, reload animation, muzzle flash, bullet tracers, shell casings eject. The spray pattern should feel like CS2 — first 5 bullets tight, then it kicks. Tap fire should be accurate at range.

2. **AWP** — bolt action sniper, scope with real zoom (not just FOV change — actual scope overlay with crosshair, blur outside the circle), breath hold for steady aim, one shot kill to body, bolt cycle between shots. The scope-in should feel weighty.

3. **Knife** — fast slash, right click stab (slower, more damage). Movement speed bonus when holding knife. This is how you rotate fast.

Number keys or scroll wheel to switch. Each weapon has a draw animation. The weapon models should look realistic — not blocky, not placeholder. Proper proportions, proper textures.

## Player mechanics

- WASD movement, shift to walk (silent), space to jump
- Crouch with ctrl (tighter spread, slower movement)
- Smooth acceleration and deceleration, not instant stop
- Crosshair that expands on movement and tightens when still
- Head bob while walking, none while scoped
- Footstep sounds on different surfaces (concrete, sand, wood, metal)
- Jump stamina — you slow after consecutive jumps like CS2
- 100 HP, damage directional indicator on screen

---

## Bot AI (4-5 bots)

This is the hardest part. The bots should not feel like NPCs walking in circles. They should feel like bad CS players:

- Bots peek corners using jiggle-peek (quick strafe out and back)
- Bots pre-aim common angles
- Bots hear footsteps and react to sound (turn toward gunshots, push if they hear you running)
- Bots take cover when shot at — they don't just stand in the open
- Bots have different aggression levels: some push, some hold angles, some camp
- Bots call out (text on screen like "A site!" "Planting!" "He's lit!")
- Bots plant the bomb if they reach the site. You have to stop them
- Bots die with ragdoll physics or at minimum a death animation
- Bots have visible weapon models and shoot back with muzzle flash

They don't need to be pro players. They need to look real in a 2-minute video. A bot peeking a corner with an AK and spraying at you looks 10x better than a bot standing still and shooting.

---

## Round structure

One round. Timer visible. Simple structure:

1. Round starts — you spawn CT side with $0 buy (just spawn with AK + knife)
2. 4-5 T bots push toward A site from different angles (long, short, catwalk)
3. You defend the site
4. If a bot reaches site, they plant the bomb (beeping sound, visual indicator)
5. You have to kill all bots OR defuse the bomb
6. Round win/loss screen
7. Option to replay

No economy. No buy menu. No multiple rounds. One round, play it, replay it. That's the scope.

## How to build this

Work on ONE system at a time in this exact order. Do NOT fan out multiple sub-agents in parallel — my machine can't handle it. Build each system sequentially:

1. **Blender map** — build the full A site in Blender via MCP. Walls, ramp, boxes, barrels, floor, elevated positions. Apply materials. Export to glTF. Get it loaded in Three.js with proper scale and colliders. This is the foundation — nothing else starts until this looks like Dust2.

2. **Player controller** — first person camera, WASD movement, crouch, jump, sprint, walk. Collision with the Blender map geometry. Footstep sounds on different surfaces. This should feel tight and responsive like CS2 movement, not floaty.

3. **Weapons system** — AK, AWP, knife. Firing, recoil, reload, weapon switching. Muzzle flash, tracers, shell casings. Crosshair that reacts to movement and firing. Each weapon should feel different. AWP scope overlay. Test extensively — the shooting feel is everything.

4. **Hit detection and damage** — raycasting from weapon to world. Headshot/body/leg multipliers. Bullet holes as decals on surfaces. Impact effects (sparks, dust, splinters). Blood on hit. HP system. Death state.

5. **Bot AI** — pathfinding on the Blender navmesh. 4-5 bots that spawn T side and push toward site. Jiggle peeks, pre-aiming, reacting to sound, taking cover. Different aggression levels. Bots shoot back with visible weapons and muzzle flash. Death animations. This is the system that makes or breaks the video.

6. **Bomb mechanic** — bot reaches site, plays plant animation, bomb appears on ground. Beeping starts. Timer (40 seconds). Player can defuse (hold E for 5 seconds). Bomb explodes if timer runs out. Win condition: all bots dead or bomb defused.

7. **HUD and game flow** — crosshair, HP, ammo, round timer, kill feed, minimap/radar. Round start countdown, round end screen (win/loss), replay button. Clean CS2-style layout. Don't over-design — functional and readable.

8. **Sound and post-processing** — layer all sounds. Ambient wind. Weapon audio. Footsteps. Bot callouts. Bomb beeps. Add post-processing: slight color grading (warm desert tones), subtle depth of field when scoped, screen shake on explosions. No bloom. No lens flare. Clean and sharp.

## Constraints the project was built under

Set by the user at the start and kept throughout (verified against the code where possible):

- Single-player only; no multiplayer, no networking code exists.
- No buy menu, no menus at all: `index.html` has one overlay with a "CLICK TO PLAY" button; every option is a URL parameter or a console call (`__game.cheats.*`).
- Exactly three weapons: `src/gameplay/WeaponDefs.js` defines `ak47`, `awp`, `knife` and nothing else. The loadout is fixed every round (`Weapons.resetLoadout`).
- One bombsite (A). One `BOMBSITE_A` empty in `tools/build_map.py`; the zone radius comes from its scale.
- No grenades. No grenade code exists.
- No music: `src/systems/Audio.js` has ambience loops, one-shots, voice lines and synthesized UI tones only.
- No placeholder boxes: every prop in the map is authored in `build_map.py` (crates have planks, gaps, bevels; barrels, rubble, the pickup truck, etc.).
- Bots must peek and take cover: `Bot.js` has `advance / peek / engage / retreat / hunt / plant / defend / defuse / dead` states, with `COVER_*` empties from Blender and a jiggle-peek routine.
- Weapon feel was treated as critical: CS2 `weapons.vdata` inaccuracy/recovery numbers, a hand-authored AK pattern, aim-punch vs view-punch split, spring-based viewmodel kick, bolt/reload procedural animation.
- Target: 60 fps on an RTX 4060 at 1080p.
- No ripped content: the user explicitly refused the Source2Viewer route; nothing was exported from CS2. Reference material is limited to screenshots in `_ref/` (six `cs2_user_clip_*.jpg` frames supplied by the user plus three web images of Dust II used for colour/mood comparison), which are never loaded by the game.

## Architecture overview

Entry: `index.html` -> `src/main.js` -> `Game` (`src/core/Game.js`).

```
src/
  core/      Game.js (loop, warm-up, render orchestration), Constants.js, RenderScale.js, EventBus.js, GameState.js
  level/     MapLoader.js, Sky.js, Dust.js, Atmosphere.js, Decals.js, Grime.js, ShadowFilter.js, ShadowDepth.js
  gameplay/  Player.js, Weapons.js, WeaponDefs.js, WeaponTextures.js, ViewModel.js, Combat.js, Bomb.js, Round.js, Spotter.js, Cheats.js
  ai/        Bot.js, BotManager.js, NavGraph.js, Difficulty.js, Cloth.js
  fx/        PostFX.js, Impacts.js, Particles.js, Shells.js, MuzzleFlash.js
  systems/   Input.js, Audio.js, Sfx.js, FlyCamera.js
  ui/        HUD.js, Radar.js, Crosshair.js, WeaponIcons.js, hud.css
```

Systems communicate through a tiny `EventBus` (`ROUND_START`, `WEAPON_FIRE`, `HIT`, `KILL`, `BOT_CALLOUT`, `BOMB_PLANTED`, ...) and read a single shared `state` object (`GameState.js`) that the HUD renders from. `window.__game` exposes the `Game` instance for debugging and for the Playwright harnesses; `window.__view(x,y,z,yaw,pitch)` swaps to a fly camera for review screenshots.

Frame order (`Game._frame`): timer -> render-scale GPU query begin -> player -> weapons -> FOV/scope -> round -> bots -> spotter -> bomb -> HUD -> sun follow / shadow dirty check -> dust, atmosphere, shells, particles, impacts -> sfx -> viewmodel update -> post-processed world render -> viewmodel render on top (depth cleared) -> query end.

## Asset pipeline

### Blender: how it was driven

Two modes were used; both are visible in the scripts.

1. Through the Blender MCP add-on (`execute_blender_code`). `tools/build_map.py` still carries its original run instruction at the top: `exec(open(r"C:\Code\cs2-dust2\tools\build_map.py").read())` "executed inside Blender (via MCP)". The bot builder has the equivalent `exec(compile(...))` line. Screenshots for review were not taken with the viewport screenshot tool (those came back black); instead every script ends by creating a Sun light, a World background and a camera (`build_map.py` "preview lighting (not exported)": `Sun`, `World`, `Cam` at ramp-top looking at the site; `build_bots_human.py` preserves `RCAM` / `RSUN` objects across rebuilds) so a still could be rendered to JPG and read back.
2. Background mode (`blender -b --python ...`), adopted later so a rebuild would not steal the user's mouse/focus. Evidence: `build_map.py` has a `BUILD_CUT_LOG` environment variable for boolean-cut timing logs, and the comment "every operator call re-evaluates the whole depsgraph, which made the ~5000-object build O(n^2) (an hour in background mode)" explaining why the map builder was rewritten to use `bmesh` / data API instead of `bpy.ops` primitives. `_tmp_blender/` (empty now) is the scratch dir the bot script creates. `tools/dust2_a.blend` (+ `.blend1`) is a saved copy of the built map scene.

Asset integrations of the Blender MCP add-on (Hyper3D Rodin, Sketchfab, Poly Pizza, Hunyuan3D) were checked and were disabled/unavailable; no model from any of them exists in the repo. The Poly Haven integration is a separate question: Poly Haven textures were used, but they were pulled with a plain Python script against `api.polyhaven.com` (`tools/fetch_textures.py`), not through the MCP `download_polyhaven_asset` tool.

The final export of the map is not inside `build_map.py` (it ends at the `BUILD OK objects/tris` print). The GLB was written by a separate `bpy.ops.export_scene.gltf` call issued interactively; the intended output path is recorded as `OUT_GLB = assets_src/dust2_a.glb` (85 MB source export). The weapon, prop and bot scripts export from within the script.

### tools/build_map.py (1,673 lines) — the A-site

Coordinate convention: Blender X east, Y north, Z up in metres; glTF export converts to Y-up. Levels: site 0.0, short/catwalk +2.5, pit -2.4, CT spawn -3.5, long doors / T side at 0.

- Materials: 16 Poly Haven texture sets wired to Principled BSDF nodes (`tex_mat`), each with custom properties `uv_scale`, `surface` (concrete / sand / wood / metal, read by the game for footsteps, impacts and bullet-hole look), `cyl` (cylindrical projection for barrels), optional `tint` (runtime base-colour factor: painted teal/ochre plaster bands, the chalky blue pickup) and `nocollide` (skyline/hills/wires). Flat materials for shutters, glass, dark interiors, wires, rubber, white metal, canvas awnings.
- Geometry helpers: `add_box` (bevelled cubes), `add_mesh`, `add_wedge` (ramps), cylinders, exact-solver boolean `cut` with box/cylinder cutters, `arch_cut_y` for arched openings. Everything goes through `bmesh`/`from_pydata` and `matrix_world` writes so bounds are never stale.
- Architecture: `building(...)` (walls with stone skirt, parapet, beam row), `window(...)` with recessed reveal, sill, lintel, bars, shutters (green/red, styles ajar/closed/broken/grille/boarded, arched variants), `blind_arch`, `awning`, `doorway` (blue plank doors, steps), `string_course`, `plaster_patch` (exposed brick), `crack`, `pilaster`, `chamfer`, `vigas` (roof beams), `scupper`, `merlons`, `roof_room`, `tarp_roll`, `tv_antenna`, `fabric_awning`, `bench`, `sandbags`, `ladder`, `handcart`, `lantern`, `kerb_line`, `manhole`, `drain_grate`, `balcony`, `insulated_wire`, `dish`, `antenna`, `roofbox`, `water_tank`, `pole`, `wire` (catenaries), `pipe`, `wall_lamp`, `pallet`, `ac_unit`, `plank_stack`.
- Props: `crate` (plank crates with per-plank gaps, two plank textures), `barrel` (white/green rusty), `rubble`, `pickup_truck` (profile-lofted body, cab, bed, wheels, glass), the bombsite "A" sign (`sign_a.jpg`, a 512x512 hazard-striped plate with a stencilled A; the small script that drew it is not in the repo).
- Layout: floors for site, long, pit, CT, ramp, short; buildings B_north (long/A backboard), B_east, B_south, B_southwest, B_west, pit wall, CT south/west, T spawn, short; long doors (arched double door, open) and short doors; the goose nook; painted bands sitting 2 cm proud of walls (a z-fighting fix is documented in the comment).
- Decals as data: `decal(kind, pos, normal_yaw, w, h, cell, alpha)` creates `DECAL_*` empties with custom properties; `face_decal` places them on a wall face. Kinds: streak/splash/pocks/stain (grime), poster/sign/posters/sign2 (posters), tag/stencil/script/marks (graffiti).
- Gameplay markers: `SPAWN_CT`, `SPAWN_T_0..4`, `BOMBSITE_A` (scale 7 = radius), 30 `NAV_*` empties (long_t ... ct_spawn, short_t ... pit), 12 `COVER_*` empties with a yaw (peek direction). Street-level detail is clearance-checked against nav edges (`track`/`untrack`, `NAV_CLEAR`) so nothing blocks bot routes.
- Finishing: `box_uv` (world-space triplanar box projection at each material's `uv_scale`; cylindrical for barrels; planar for the sign), `smooth_sharp` (auto-smooth by 32 degrees), then a merge-by-material pass that joins all objects sharing a material into one `MAP_<material>` mesh (draw-call reduction; the `surface` and `nocollide` props ride along). The result is 40-ish meshes instead of ~5,000 objects.
- Export: the 85 MB `assets_src/dust2_a.glb` was post-processed with `gltf-transform` (`webp`) into `public/models/dust2_a.webp.glb` (33.8 MB), which is what the game loads (`Constants.ASSETS.MAP`).

### tools/build_weapons.py (770 lines) — AK-47, AWP, knife, gloved hands

- Blender +Y = barrel forward, origin at the grip. Named nodes the game drives: `Muzzle`, `Eject`, `Mag`, `HandL/R`, `ArmL/R`, `ScopeLens`, `bolt_knob`.
- Primitives plus a `loft` of rounded-rectangle cross sections (`rprof`, `ring_xz`, `ring_xy`) for receivers, handguards, grips; tapered cylinders for barrels and gas tubes.
- Hands: parametric gloved hand (`hand()`) with three-phalanx `finger()` lofts, thumb with CMC/MCP joints, padded knuckles, glove cuff; `arm_bent` sleeves. Three thumb poses (`THUMB_OPEN`, `THUMB_SIDE`, `THUMB_LOCK`). Palm faces get a separate `glove_palm` (leather) material chosen by hand-local normal.
- AWP: scope tube with lens discs given their own 0..1 UVs (`lens_uv`) so the JS lens texture maps cleanly; bolt knob node for the procedural bolt cycle.
- Knife: lofted handle, pommel, lanyard, guard, a `blade()` with a polished `knife_edge` strip, and a hammer grip; the idle pose is designed in camera space and converted with `three_rot` so `WeaponDefs.knife.view.rot` matches exactly (`[1.112, 0.582, 0.999]`).
- Materials are flat Principled colours whose names (`gun_steel`, `gun_wood`, `gun_bakelite`, `awp_green`, `scope_glass`, `knife_blade`, `glove`, `glove_palm`, `sleeve`, ...) are the contract for `ViewModel._dress` and `WeaponTextures.js`.
- Edge-wear mask: bevel strips are tagged with a temporary `_wearmark` material and baked into `COLOR_0.r` (`mark_bevel` / `bake_wear`), which the runtime turns into worn bare metal along edges.
- Box-projected UVs at fixed texel density (`box_uv`; wood gets `wood_uv`). Export: GLB, no images, vertex colours on, extras on. Sizes: ak47.glb 640 KB, awp.glb 670 KB, knife.glb 205 KB.

### tools/build_bots_human.py (1,400 lines) — T/CT bots as humans

Route (from the docstring, all confirmed by the code):

1. Import `assets_src/characters/Soldier.glb` (the three.js example "Soldier", a Mixamo Vanguard rig) for its exact armature and its Idle / Walk / Run / TPose actions; the Vanguard meshes are deleted. The rest pose is corrected: neck +26 mm, head +74 mm, upper arms x1.42, palm 0.70 / phalanges 1.32 (measured in-game deficiencies, animation-safe because Mixamo keys rotation only).
2. MPFB2 (`bl_ext.blender_org.mpfb`) generates a male base mesh with a `MACRO` dict (age 0.55, muscle 0.72, proportions 0.62, mixed ethnicity) and the built-in `mixamo_unity` rig with MakeHuman skin weights; helper geometry is stripped, the shipped A-to-T pose correction is applied as rest.
3. `retarget()` poses the MPFB rig bone-by-bone onto the Soldier rest pose and bakes the deformation, so the human body wears the game skeleton.
4. Decimation to 36 % with per-region density (`DENSITY`: head 0.62, hands kept at ~1,400 tris).
5. Clothing is pushed out of the body per bone-dominated region (`BODY_REGIONS` -> `GARMENT` offsets: shirt, cargo trousers, boots, balaclava, gloves), with Laplacian smoothing of the thickness, `carve_neck`, `tuck_ears`, `relax`. Gear props (plate carrier, pouches, belt, head gear: balaclava / cap / shemagh / goggles) are built from primitives and shrinkwrapped, with weights transferred by KD-tree.
6. Four variants (`mask`, `mask_cap`, `wrap`, `wrap_goggles`) with a `PALETTE` designed for value contrast at range (the comment explains why: at 30 m a bot is ~60 px tall and mips to its mean colour).
7. Procedural cloth materials per garment key (`SURFACE`: ripstop grid, cargo cloth, MOLLE nylon, leather grain, knit rib) are baked with Cycles into a 2048 albedo/roughness/normal atlas per variant, with AO baked into `COLOR_0`; everything joins into one skinned `bot_cloth` mesh, 4 weights per vertex.
8. Export: GLB with skins and NLA-track animations, WebP textures at q82 (`export_image_format='WEBP'`). Sizes 1.55-1.64 MB each.

Two earlier bot builders remain for history: `build_bot.py` (segmented Elite-Crew-style mannequin for purely procedural animation) and `build_bots_rigged.py` (the Vanguard/Soldier mesh re-tinted with accessories; `BASE_HAS_HELMET = True` comments that the trooper wears a helmet). The Vanguard look was rejected as "sci-fi, not CS", which led to the MPFB2 route. `assets_src/anim/README.txt` describes how to drop Mixamo FBX clips in; none were added — the folder holds only the README, so the bots animate with Idle/Walk/Run and procedural layers for everything else.

### Textures — what is procedural and what is not

- Poly Haven CC0 scans (map only): beige_wall_002, worn_plaster_wall, plaster_stone_wall_01, stone_wall_03, sandy_gravel, cobblestone_04, dry_ground_01, concrete_pavement, worn_asphalt (2k); weathered_planks, rough_wood, wood_planks_dirt, rusty_metal_02, green_metal_rust, blue_painted_planks, corrugated_iron_02 (1k). `fetch_textures.py` downloads diffuse / normal (GL) / roughness / AO, premultiplies a softened AO into the diffuse; `grade_textures.py` normalises luminance and tints each toward the CS2 Dust II palette (off-white cream plaster, light sandstone slab, sun-bleached tan crates, warm-grey asphalt) and blends the worn plaster 62 % toward the smooth one. Cached in `tools/texcache/` (~150 MB), baked into the GLB by Blender, converted to WebP by gltf-transform.
- Generated in JS (`WeaponTextures.js`, no image files): tiling value-noise / fBm canvases for steel (with wear-noise packed in R), light steel, phosphate speckle, AWP polymer, laminate wood, bakelite, scope lens, glove knuckle fabric, glove leather, rip-stop sleeve. Normal maps derived from height. Reused for the bots' world-space AK (`BotManager.dressWorldGun`).
- Generated in GLSL/JS at load (`Grime.js`): a tileable 512 mottle canvas; shader injection into every map `MeshStandardMaterial` for three-octave world-space mottling, wall skirts, sun-bleach, rain drips, anti-tiling rotated second sample on floors, in-shader flattening of the worn plaster's own patches (divide by its mip-4 blur), sand drifts driven by a wall-proximity mask rasterised from the collider (`buildWallProximity`, 4 floor levels in RGBA).
- Other generated art: muzzle flash sprites (`MuzzleFlash.js`), bullet-hole / blood / pool atlases (`Impacts.js`), bird flap atlas (`Atmosphere.js`), radar backdrop (height scan of the collider), weapon HUD glyphs rendered from the meshes (`WeaponIcons.js`), sky (`Sky.js` shader), decal sheets (PIL, above).
- Image files under `public/textures/`: only `decals/graffiti.png`, `decals/posters.png`, `decals/grime.png`.
- AI image generation: no evidence anywhere in the repo (no Gemini / GenerateImage output, no generated PNG other than the PIL sheets). Answer: no, it was not used for shipped assets.

### Audio

- Raw sources: 36 CC0 recordings from Freesound, downloaded with `tools/fs.mjs` (searches Freesound for CC0 hits and saves the HQ preview MP3; appends the credit line). Full attribution in `public/audio/CREDITS.txt` (AK/AWP shots, Mosin bolt, AK reload, explosions, footsteps on four surfaces, impacts, headshot dinks, flyby, ricochet, brass, knife whoosh/steel/flesh, grunts, agony, body fall, wind x2, keypad, dog, crow, distant city).
- `tools/slice_audio.py`: onset detection on the RMS envelope, per-slice peak-normalise/fade/trim, then synthesis and layering: AK gets a 140->42 Hz sub-thump and a convolved outdoor tail; distant AK reports are rendered from the close shot (`distant_shot`: lowpass + long reflection wash + discrete slaps); C4 explosion gets a 90->30 Hz ringing sub and a rumble tail; three seamless ambience loops with unrelated periods (38.5 / 28.5 / 23.5 s, `loopify`); far dog/crow one-shots at two pitches. Output: 68 OGG files in `public/audio` (libvorbis), 36 more under `public/audio/voice`.
- `tools/make_voice.py`: announcer and radio lines from Windows SAPI TTS ("Microsoft David Desktop", System.Speech via PowerShell), then a DSP chain in numpy/scipy: announcer (slowed 0.93, -2 semitones, 180-6000 Hz band, compression), CT radio (300-3400 Hz band, overdrive, hiss bed gated by the carrier, squelch clicks), T bots in three pitches (-4 / -1 / +2.5 st). Each file trimmed, padded, faded, normalised to -16 LUFS with ffmpeg ebur128, peak 0.95. 36 voice OGGs (4 announcer, 5 CT, 9 T lines x 3 voices).
- Runtime (`Audio.js`): AudioContext created on first gesture; bank decoded through an OfflineAudioContext; buses sfx/world/amb/ui/voice; an "ears" stage (announcer ducking, C4 low-pass sweep) ahead of one compressor; 3D panning with distance low-pass (air absorption) and single-ray occlusion against the map BVH; synthesized UI/bomb tones. `Sfx.js` maps game events to sounds: per-surface footsteps and impacts, brass with delay, AWP bolt after the shot, hurt/death, whiz, bot callouts positional from the bot's head with one voice per bot, CT-side and T-side radio mapping, announcer queue.

## Runtime systems

### Game loop and render orchestration (`core/Game.js`, `RenderScale.js`, `Constants.js`)

- WebGLRenderer with MSAA, ACES tone mapping, sRGB output, PCF shadow map at 4096 with a 50 m half-extent ortho frustum that follows the player texel-snapped (`Sky.follow`).
- `installStablePCF()` is called before any material compiles (see Shadows below).
- `shadowMap.autoUpdate = false`; `_shadowDirty` re-renders the sun map only when the snapped frustum moved, a bot is animating/falling, a dropped rifle is in the air, or the C4 appeared/disappeared. Otherwise the previous map is reused (three would otherwise render it twice per frame because of GTAO's normal pre-pass).
- Chunked texture upload: `_uploadTextures` calls `renderer.initTexture` in ~12 ms slices while other assets are still downloading, so the map's textures are resident before the first frame.
- Shader warm-up (`_warmShaders`): shows pooled/hidden renderables (muzzle flashes, C4, holstered viewmodel weapons), un-culls everything, compiles the world with the HDR target bound and the viewmodel with the canvas bound (`compileAsync`), renders two full post frames (shadow-depth and GTAO variants), then a third frame looking into the sun so the sun-shaft mask/blur programs compile ("65 -> 67 programs, a hitch" otherwise). Records `stats.warm`, `stats.warmSync`, `stats.warmProgs`.
- Adaptive render scale (`RenderScale`): levels 1.0 / 1.25 / 1.4 independent of DPR; GPU time from `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`; step down above 13 ms, up below 9 ms for 3 s, at most one change per 5 s; `?scale=` pins it. Documented headless 1080p GPU times: 6.2 ms at 1.0, 7.2 at 1.25, 9.0 at 1.4, 10-17 at 1.5, hence the 1.4 cap.
- Per-frame exceptions are caught and logged once per second so a fault never kills `setAnimationLoop`.

### Map loading and collision (`level/MapLoader.js`)

- GLTFLoader; every mesh with `nocollide` goes to a background list (renders, no collision, no shadow casting). Materials are tuned per name (anisotropy, trilinear mips, normal scale restrained to 0.5 on plaster, metalness for barrels/iron, glass), tints from Blender extras applied, then `applyGrime` is injected.
- Collision: `StaticGeometryGenerator` merges all collidable meshes (positions only); one `MeshBVH` (leaf size 8) is the capsule and bullet collider. Each visible mesh also gets its own bounds tree for surface-type raycasts (`map.raycast` returns the hit plus `surface`).
- Markers: spawns, nav nodes, cover points (yaw -> direction), decals (kind/w/h/cell/alpha), bombsite (position + radius).

### Player (`gameplay/Player.js`, `Constants.PLAYER`)

- Capsule (radius 0.33, height 1.8 / crouch 1.25) swept against the BVH; eye 1.62 m (deliberately taller than CS2's 64u to suit a 1.8 m capsule).
- Source movement: `sv_accelerate 5.5`, `sv_friction 5.2`, `sv_stopspeed 80u`, `sv_airaccelerate 12` with the 30 u/s air wish cap, 0.25 s duck ramp, gravity 800 u/s^2, jump 5.75 m/s. Speeds 250/215/200 u/s for knife/AK/AWP, walk x0.52, crouch x0.34, AWP scoped x0.5. Tagging (`m_flFlinchVelocityModifier`-style) on hit, view punch flinch, landing dip, stride-based footsteps, death cam.

### Weapons (`gameplay/Weapons.js`, `WeaponDefs.js`)

- Numbers from CS2 `weapons.vdata`: inaccuracy tangents for stand/crouch/move/jump/fire/spread with the recover-time blending over spray bullets 2..5; RPM 600 / 41.24; damage 36 / 115; armour penetration 0.775 / 0.975; range modifier per 500u; reload 2.43 / 3.7 s; AWP bolt 1.2 s; scope FOVs converted from CS2's 4:3 figures (30.5 / 7.5 vertical) with `zoom_sensitivity_ratio 1`.
- AK spray pattern: a 30-shot cumulative table (near-straight for 3, steep climb to 4.0 degrees at shot 10, then the left drift / right hook / left / right). The comment records that the first table was ~1.8x too steep and was corrected.
- Aim punch vs view punch: bullets leave along eye angles + full punch; the camera follows `viewTrack` (0.55 AK, 0.45 AWP) so the crosshair stays put and bullets visibly climb; punch decays exp + linear after `punchHold`; pattern resets after 0.55 s.
- Inaccuracy sampled uniformly in radius in a disk (Source-style), bullets raycast against the BVH and bot hitboxes with distance falloff and the CS2 armour formula (`Combat.js`, part multipliers head 4.0 / stomach 1.25 / leg 0.75).
- AK aim-down-sights on RMB (not a CS2 feature; a modern-FPS style zoom): FOV 74 -> 62, sight alignment computed from the front post and rear leaf positions of the model.
- AWP: two scope levels, re-scope after the bolt, DOM scope overlay with mil hashes (`index.html #scope`).
- Knife: CS2 damage (40 / 65, back 90 / 180), reach 48u; hit test = line trace first, then a 0.3 m half-width hull sweep against the bot capsules (`_meleeTrace`) so a body a little off the crosshair, or one the player is standing inside, still takes the hit; the world still blocks.
- Quick switch (Q), 1/2/3, mouse wheel; dry-fire click; ammo per weapon.

### ViewModel (`gameplay/ViewModel.js`, `WeaponTextures.js`, `MuzzleFlash.js`)

- Separate camera-space scene rendered over the world with depth cleared, own lights (more fill than the world so the gloves read), own environment. FOV 66 vs the world's 74; the muzzle world position is re-projected for tracers.
- All animation is procedural: fire kick is an exact under-damped spring per axis (per-weapon omega/zeta), mouse-lag sway (up to 1.5 degrees, 80 ms), stride bob re-synced to footsteps, idle breathing, draw/holster lower, reload (mag out/in, charge) and AWP bolt cycle driving the `Mag` / `bolt_knob` nodes, ADS pose blend.
- `_dress` assigns the canvas texture sets by material name and installs the edge-wear shader (`addEdgeWear`: `COLOR_0.r` curvature x wear noise -> bare metal, lower roughness).
- Muzzle flash: camera-space sprite with 4 generated variants, random roll/scale, two-frame life, plus a point light in the world.

### Combat, hitboxes, impacts (`Combat.js`, `Bot.js`, `fx/Impacts.js`, `Particles.js`, `Shells.js`)

- Bot hitboxes are capsules between bone pairs, fitted once per bot to the posed skin (85th-percentile perpendicular distance, capped per part) because the sleeves/vest/boots sit off the bones; a `sweep` variant inflates them for the knife.
- `Impacts.js`: instanced decal pools with atlas tiles and per-instance tint; bullet holes per surface (`HOLE` table: 4 cm on plaster/sand, 3.5 cm wood, 2.5 cm metal, with a minimum on-screen size so holes stay readable at 10 m), blood splats, growing blood pools under settled corpses, debris particles, tracers (32, additive on colour only so the HDR alpha sky mask survives), muzzle smoke.
- `Particles.js`: one CPU-simulated 1,200-point pool (dust, sparks, blood, smoke). `Shells.js`: 48 instanced brass casings with gravity and floor bounce (bounce triggers the brass sound).

### Bot AI (`ai/Bot.js` 1,180 lines, `BotManager.js`, `NavGraph.js`, `Difficulty.js`, `Cloth.js`)

- Team: five bots (`Cliffe`, `Ryan`, `Chet`, `Wolf`, `Vitaliy`), variants mask/wrap/mask_cap/wrap_goggles/mask, three behaviour profiles (rusher / standard / lurker: reaction windows, aim error, burst lengths, peek chance, hold times, retreat HP). `buildFor(i)` derives deterministic physique/mannerism knobs per slot from a golden-ratio sequence (height 1.71-1.89 m, shoulders, bulk, clip tempo, sway, dirt).
- Navigation: `NavGraph` = the 30 `NAV_*` empties plus a hand-authored edge list, Dijkstra with avoid-sets; cover points are attached to their nearest node.
- States: `advance` (route along long / short toward the site), `peek` (approach cover -> wait -> jiggle out 0.55 m to the side -> back, n jiggles, then hold, lurkers crouch), `engage` (stop, aim with decaying error and per-difficulty time constants, bursts and pauses, strafe, crouch bias), `retreat` (below profile HP), `hunt` (go to last seen position), `plant` (T: 3.2 s in the zone), `defend` (CT: `CT_HOLDS` node/face assignments, two patrollers rotate), `defuse` (CT on the planted C4, fights first only if pressured), `dead` (death clip if present else procedural fall, dropped rifle with physics and settle).
- Perception: two LOS rays per bot at 20 Hz staggered across the squad, 75 degree half-FOV (or within 4 m, or recently seen), 70 m range, hearing on gunfire; reaction time rolled per contact; callouts through `BotManager.callout` -> `BOT_CALLOUT` -> positional voice lines.
- Difficulty (`Difficulty.js`): `easy` (default) / `normal` / `hard` table (reaction, aim error multipliers, aim and error time constants, burst sizes and pauses, spread cone, headshot probability, over-peek chance, flinch). Selected by `?bots=`, `localStorage['cs2.botdiff']`, or `cheats.difficulty()`; read live so a mid-round switch applies at the next contact.
- Animation: `AnimationMixer` on the Soldier clips (idle/walk/run, clip speed scaled to actual velocity), with procedural layers on top: aim yaw/pitch split across Spine/Spine1/Spine2/Head, bladed stance, two-hand rifle IK (right hand on the grip, support hand on the handguard with reach-designed anchors for aim / low-ready / run carries), finger curling into a rifle grip, idle sway, fallback crouch (pelvis drop + leg fold) because the rig has no crouch clip, procedural death fall.
- Sides: when the player is T, the same bots play CT. `Cloth.js` is a shader patch on the baked atlas material: `uCT` remaps every non-skin pixel to a luminance-preserving blue-grey (skin kept by hue ratio) plus weave/dust; it is a uniform, not a define, so T and CT compile to one program. `BotManager._matSet` caches material sets per (variant, slot, side) so respawns never re-clone materials; `shadowDirty` tells `Game` when bots or dropped rifles need a shadow refresh. Bot rifles are the first-person AK GLB without hands, dressed by `dressWorldGun`.
- The player-side counterpart, `Spotter.js`, runs a 0.2 s frustum + LOS check to emit `PLAYER_SPOT` for the CT "enemy spotted" radio.

### Bomb and round (`gameplay/Bomb.js`, `Round.js`, `Cheats.js`)

- C4: 40 s timer, 3.2 s plant, 5 s defuse (kit), LED blink and beeps at the CS2 cadence, blast radius 26 m with falloff to player and bots, screen flash + shake + muffled-ears sweep. One point light for LED and blast kept visible at intensity 0 so no shader recompile happens at plant time (a 1.6 s stall was measured and fixed this way). Player plant (T side or `cheats.bomb()`), player defuse (hold E, in range, looking at it), dropped C4 where a carrier fell, bot pick-up.
- Round: 1:55, 3 s freeze, 7 s end hold; CT-side and T-side win conditions as in CS (a dead planter still wins on detonation; killing every CT after the plant ends the round). Sides alternate every round (CS half-time in miniature); `?side=t|ct` pins; `cheats.side()` sets the next side and stores it. Explore mode (`?explore=1`): no bots, no freeze, frozen timer, god mode.

### HUD, radar, crosshair (`ui/*`)

- DOM HUD in the CS2 layout: radar top-left, score/timer/alive ticks top-centre, killfeed top-right, HP/armour bottom-left, ammo + weapon glyph bottom-right, damage direction wedges, hit flash, low-HP vignette, bomb hint (HOLD E TO DEFUSE / PLANT), defuse/plant bar, objective line (DEFEND / PLANT THE BOMB AT A), death panel, win panel, C4 carry icon. Font: Barlow Semi Condensed 500-800 (`public/fonts`).
- Radar (`Radar.js`): backdrop baked once from a top-down height scan of the collider (664 px over a 70 m half-extent around the site, ~0.21 m/px, walkable heights light, sliced across idle time so it never stalls a frame), rotating with the player, spotted enemies (LOS / recent gunfire / very close), planted C4, bombsite letter. The comment on the player arrow documents the mirrored-heading bug that was fixed.
- Crosshair (`Crosshair.js`): full Valorant "Import Profile Code" parser (sections P/A/S, colours incl. custom `u;RRGGBBAA`, outlines, centre dot, inner/outer lines, movement and firing error), drawn on a DPR-aware canvas; `?xhair=`, `localStorage['cs2.xhair']`, `cheats.xhair(code)`.
- Weapon glyphs are white side-view silhouettes rendered from the real meshes at load (`WeaponIcons.js`).

### Sky, atmosphere, dust, decals (`level/Sky.js`, `Atmosphere.js`, `Dust.js`, `Decals.js`)

- `DesertSky`: procedural dome shader (zenith/horizon gradient, dust band, sun disc + corona + mie glow, fBm cirrus, dither), writes alpha 0 so the post pipeline has an MSAA-resolved sky mask; DirectionalLight sun (ESE, ~39 degrees elevation, 4800 K, intensity 5.6) + HemisphereLight fill at ~8:1; FogExp2 whose colour is derived from the dome's horizon; PMREM environment from a sun-less copy of the dome.
- `Dust`: 1,000 GPU point motes in a camera-following box that wrap in world space, each testing the sun shadow map in the vertex shader so they glitter only in sunlight.
- `Atmosphere`: seven far swifts (Points with a 4-frame flap atlas), catenary sway on the `MAP_wire` mesh, wind-blown litter (InstancedMesh), foot dust for player and bots.
- `Decals`: the `DECAL_*` markers become quads reading a 2x2 cell of the grime (multiply), graffiti (multiply, keyed) or posters (alpha-keyed from white, lit `MeshStandardMaterial`) sheets, materials cached per (sheet, alpha).

### Shadows (`level/ShadowFilter.js`, `ShadowDepth.js`)

- `installStablePCF` replaces three r185's directional `getShadow()`: its per-screen-pixel rotated Vogel disk is a TAA-style noise that this renderer has no TAA to average, so every penumbra and every grazing-lit wall shimmered while moving (measured: the shadow term alone changed wall luminance by 5.8/255 per frame on a 1 px strafe). The replacement uses a fixed-orientation 8-tap Vogel disk (x 4-tap hardware bilinear = 32 effective taps) and a receiver-plane depth bias (Isidoro 2006) with a clamped gradient, so flat lit surfaces never self-shadow however grazing the sun. `?pcf=stock` restores three's filter for A/B.
- `assignDepthMaterials` gives each (material, skinned, instanced) tuple its own `MeshDepthMaterial` so the shadow pass stops re-running `getProgram` per caster (~7 % CPU and ~190 MB / 10 s of garbage before).

### Post-processing (`fx/PostFX.js`)

EffectComposer on a HalfFloat 4x MSAA target: RenderPass -> GTAO (own normal+depth G-buffer at 0.75 scale, radius 1.1, Poisson denoise) -> SunShaftsPass (mask from the sky alpha weighted by sun proximity, 28-step radial blur, half res) -> UnrealBloom (threshold 2.6 so only sun / flashes / blast bleed) -> OutputPass (ACES + sRGB) -> grade (unsharp mask, S-curve contrast 0.32, saturation 0.98, depth-driven aerial desaturation between 25 and 150 m using GTAO's depth, cool-shadow / warm-highlight split tone, vignette 0.20, grain 0.014, C4 white-out). URL A/B switches: `?aa=msaa|smaa|none`, `?ao=normal|depth`, `?aoscale=`, `?aosamples=`, `?shaftscale=`. The comments record why SMAA and depth-fed GTAO were not shipped.

### Input (`systems/Input.js`, `main.js`)

- Raw pointer: `requestPointerLock({ unadjustedMovement: true })` with fallbacks; CS2 sensitivity formula (`deg = counts x sens x 0.022`), default 2.0, `?sens=`, `localStorage['cs2.sens']`, `cheats.sens()`; scaled while scoped / in ADS.
- Play = fullscreen + pointer lock + `navigator.keyboard.lock()` so Ctrl+W / Ctrl+1..8 stay in the game (Ctrl is the walk key). Esc releases everything and the overlay returns.
- Binds live in `Input.BINDS`: crouch = Shift (or C), walk = Ctrl; noclip fly uses Space/Ctrl up/down and Shift fast.
- Automation guard: under `navigator.webdriver` or `?nolock=1` no fullscreen, pointer lock or keyboard lock is requested; `forceLocked` makes the game believe it is locked. This exists because headless runs on Windows were hiding/confining/re-centring the user's real mouse (see the fixes section).

## Performance work

Documented in code comments and constants:

- Draw-call reduction by merging the map per material in Blender; static BVH collider; instanced decals, shells, litter; one Points draw for particles, dust, birds.
- Shadow-map refresh gating, per-caster depth materials, texel-snapped frustum, 4096 map with 2.4 cm texels, normalBias 0.018.
- Shader warm-up before the overlay is dismissed, including the sun-shaft programs and the HDR-target variants (ANGLE/D3D11 compiles a PBR program in ~140 ms synchronously at first use).
- No light appearing at runtime (C4 light always present), uniform-based CT recolour (no extra program), material-set caching per bot slot.
- GTAO at 0.75 scale, sun shafts at 0.5 scale, bloom on a half-res chain; MSAA kept over SMAA.
- Chunked texture upload; radar height scan sliced across idle time; bot LOS rays staggered at 20 Hz.
- Adaptive render scale with GPU timer queries.

Numbers that were reported during the sessions and are not stored in the repo (treat as session measurements, not reproducible artefacts): about 16.7 ms/frame at 1080p on the RTX 4060 (i.e. the 60 fps target), ~371 draw calls, ~845k triangles, 65-67 shader programs, 124-126 textures, load time ~5-6 s. The repo corroborates parts of this: `Game.js` mentions the 65 -> 67 program count, `RenderScale.js` lists the headless 1080p GPU frame times per scale level, and `tools/shots.mjs` / `fpstest.mjs` are the scripts that read `renderer.info.render.calls/triangles` and fps.

## Timeline of feedback-driven fixes

Each of these is traceable to a comment in the code:

- Flicker while moving -> `ShadowFilter.js`: three's per-pixel rotated PCF replaced by a fixed Vogel disk with receiver-plane bias; measured before/after on the short-corridor wall.
- Urdu/Arabic text on posters and graffiti -> `make_decals.py` regenerated the sheets with numerals and pictograms only; no letters anywhere.
- Bullet holes too big ("paintball splats at 1.5 m") -> `Impacts.HOLE` sizes cut to 2.5-4 cm with a minimum on-screen size so they still read at distance.
- Headless tests hijacking the user's mouse -> `main.js` / `Input.js` automation guard (`navigator.webdriver` / `?nolock=1`, `forceLocked`), no fullscreen/pointer/keyboard lock under automation.
- FPS drops while test browsers shared the GPU -> harnesses are run one at a time; the adaptive render scale ignores the first 1.5 s and only reacts to sustained GPU time. (The GPU-sharing observation itself is a session note, not recorded in code.)
- Knife doing no damage -> `Weapons._meleeTrace`: line trace then 0.3 m hull sweep, and `Bot.sweep` treating an origin inside the capsule as a hit.
- Radar arrow mirrored (pointed the opposite way down the corridors) -> `Radar.js` rotation sign fix, documented inline.
- Knife HUD showing "undefined" ammo -> HUD ammo row hides for melee and `Weapons.def/ammo` getters fall back safely; `WeaponDefs.knife` carries a full `inacc` block so nothing dereferences undefined.
- T side + objective hints -> `Round.js` side alternation, `?side=`, `cheats.side()`, CT bots with `CT_HOLDS`, the CT recolour uniform in `Cloth.js`, the HUD objective line ("DEFEND BOMBSITE A" / "PLANT THE BOMB AT A") and the plant verb on the bomb hint.
- Bots looked like sci-fi troopers -> MPFB2 human bodies replaced the Vanguard mesh (`build_bots_rigged.py` -> `build_bots_human.py`).
- Bots read as single-colour mannequins at range -> value-contrast palette per kit.
- AK spray too steep -> pattern table scaled down ~1.8x.
- Plaza read orange -> sun colour cooled from 0xffdcb0 to 0xffe6c8, floor albedo tints neutralised, saturation grade lowered.
- Plaster read as gravel up close -> normal scale 0.5 on walls; worn plaster's peel patches flattened in-shader.
- C4 plant caused a 1.6 s stall -> the bomb light is always in the scene at intensity 0.
- First hit / death cam stalled 100-460 ms -> shader warm-up with the HDR target bound and everything un-culled.
- Painted band z-fighting -> bands moved 2 cm proud of the wall plane.
- Coplanar mesh boundary between stone skirt and plaster too clean -> `Grime.js` band frays.

## Known limitations and things not done

- Only the A-site slice of Dust II exists (long, short/catwalk, pit, site, ramp, CT spawn, T spawn at long doors). No B, no mid, no tunnels.
- Bot animation is limited to Idle/Walk/Run clips plus procedural layers; there is no authored crouch, aim or death clip (the README in `assets_src/anim` explains how to add Mixamo clips, none were added).
- No multiplayer, buy menu, economy, grenades, armour purchase (the player always spawns with 100 armour and a helmet), no settings menu, no music.
- The map source GLB (85 MB) and the Poly Haven cache (~150 MB) are large; the runtime GLB is 33.8 MB and the total load is several seconds.
- The map's surface textures are CC0 scans and the sound one-shots are CC0 recordings, not original art (the geometry they sit on is); `public/audio/CREDITS.txt` and the `fetch_textures.py` list are the record.
- The `sign_a.jpg` generator and the `grime.png` generator are not in the repo; the map export step is not in `build_map.py`.
- Perf figures were measured on one machine (RTX 4060, Windows, Chromium on ANGLE/D3D11) and are not reproducible from the repo.
- Firefox is only partially supported (no keyboard lock, `unadjustedMovement` fallback).
- The Valorant crosshair parser ignores the sniper section; the AWP uses the DOM scope.
- No save/progress: score persists only within a page session.
